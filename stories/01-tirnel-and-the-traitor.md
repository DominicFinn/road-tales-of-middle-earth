# The Messenger of the Sudden Flame

*Beleriand, First Age. The Dagor Bragollach and after.*

## The fire on Ard-galen

Tirnel was a young Sindarin scout who rode for Fingolfin's watch-posts on the edge of the plain of Ard-galen. She was the first to see it. The sky above Thangorodrim glowed red, and rivers of flame came rolling down across the grassland faster than any horse could run. Her watch-post had one duty if the worst came: get a warning to Barad Eithel.

Her horse died under her in the smoke before she'd gone three leagues. She carried on through the burning grass on foot, and at dawn, stumbling into a gully, she heard orcs. Dozens of them, guarding a captured Elven messenger-hawk, its message tube still on its leg.

> *The player chose: she goes for the message.*

She waited for the smoke to thicken, crept down, silenced a guard and cut the hawk free. It screamed, the camp roared, and she ran with it clutched to her chest, dropping into a stream and letting the current carry her away under the roots.

The message wasn't Elvish. It was written in Elvish script, but in the hand of someone serving Morgoth, to a traitor inside Barad Eithel. It described a hidden postern gate on the western wall, to be left unbarred at moonrise on the third night.

## The trap at the postern

> *The player chose: she asks to speak directly to someone of importance.*

Tirnel reached Barad Eithel two days later, starved and black with soot, and refused to give her message to anyone but the lord of the fortress. Fingolfin was on the walls of the Ered Wethrin, so it was Fingon who came. She told him of the fire, then asked to speak alone, and handed him the letter.

"Tell no one," he said. "The postern is known to fewer than ten people in this fortress."

Fingon set a trap. At moonrise the bolt slid back from the inside, and the lantern lit a face Tirnel knew: Ulgar, an Easterling who'd come the summer before as a refugee and been made a groom in the stables.

Ulgar smiled sadly. "You're too late, my lord. The gate was never the plan." A horn blew from the eastern wall. The letter was a decoy, written to be found, to draw Fingon's best warriors west while the real attack came east. As Fingon ran, Ulgar whispered to Tirnel that more of his people were coming from beyond the Blue Mountains, "and your lords will welcome them as friends".

## The eastern wall

> *The player chose: carry on with the battle.*

Orcs were on the battlements and fighting towards the winch of the main gate. Tirnel climbed onto the gatehouse roof, tipped a vat of oil onto them and threw a torch after it. The orcs broke, the defenders rallied to cries of "Fingon!", and by dawn Barad Eithel still stood, as it does in the histories.

But Ulgar's guards were dead, and he was gone.

## The broken hero

> *The player asked: how did a stable groom know about the postern?*

Fingon questioned the ten who knew of the gate. Nobody cracked. But Tirnel noticed that one of them, Gelmir, an Elf who had escaped from Angband and been welcomed home a hero, rode out every morning, and every morning Ulgar saddled his horse. Morgoth sometimes let thralls "escape" with poisoned minds. Gelmir had passed secrets in those quiet stable moments and couldn't remember doing it.

> *The player chose: tell Gelmir the truth, and let him avenge himself.*

Fingon told him gently. Gelmir laughed, then went grey as memories surfaced: a voice in the dark of Angband, burning eyes, a whisper telling him to forget. "Then let me undo it," he said.

He rode out alone as before, shadowed by Tirnel and Fingon's riders. Three mornings later Ulgar stepped out of the trees with a dozen Easterlings. "Come, friend. Our master wants you home."

"I'm already home," said Gelmir, and charged.

Fingon's riders broke from the trees. Tirnel took an axe-blow on her shield. In the end it was Gelmir who faced Ulgar alone among the pines. "He'll just take you again," Ulgar hissed. "Not while I remember," said Gelmir, and struck him down. Dying, Ulgar laughed that his people were already crossing the mountains.

## The Nirnaeth

Years later Tirnel rode with Fingon's host at the Battle of Unnumbered Tears. She saw the Easterlings under Ulfang turn on the sons of Fëanor, as Ulgar had promised. She saw Fingon fall to Gothmog. In the rout she was swept to the Fen of Serech, where Huor pushed the reins into her hand: "Ride. Someone must live to tell this." The last thing she saw was Húrin on a mound of fallen trolls, crying "Aurë entuluva!"

She reached the Havens of Círdan weeks later and spent the rest of the age writing down what she'd seen, so that Fingon, Gelmir, Húrin and Huor would never be forgotten.
