# The Lake That Doesn't Freeze

*The far north, Second Age. Pallando's journey after the pact with Alatar. This was the first story played choose-your-own-adventure style.*

## Chapter one: the road north

Pallando crossed the grasslands, paying for bread with stories and small healings. In every camp, sons had been called north by priests promising glory. In the tent of an old herdsman, Batu, he met Batu's grandson Temur, who'd come back from the north alone and half frozen, with no memory, muttering in his sleep about "the lake that doesn't freeze". Pallando saw a dark shimmer in his eyes. A thread of the mirror still held him, and a thread runs both ways.

"I'd rather face it than listen to it for the rest of my life," Temur said, and went north with him.

## Chapter two: the ice people

After two months, at the edge of the ice, Hiru hunters rose out of the snow with spears levelled. The last strangers from the south had been mirror-priests.

> *The player chose: let Temur speak.*

Temur pulled back his hood. "The priests took me last winter. I knelt there. I still hear it now." Spears lowered. A huntress with two fingers missing, Kiri, looked into his eyes. "That boy's been to the lake." Her husband and brother had been taken two winters before. She'd killed three priests and tried to reach the lake four times, and each time woke up walking the wrong way.

The elder set a test: three children in the camp had stopped eating and stared north all day.

> *The player chose: let Temur sit with them first.*

Temur hummed the mirror's own tune, slower and slightly wrong, teasing it. "It sounds like someone who loves you. But it's lying. I went to it, and there was nobody there." The little girl cried a real child's cry, and Pallando's staff snapped the threads. But the children had never been near the lake. The mirror's reach was growing.

Kiri told Pallando privately of a priests' camp two days north, with prisoners. Perhaps her brother.

> *The player chose: go straight for the mirror. Breaking it frees everyone anyway.*

Kiri didn't take it well, but in the morning she was waiting with her spear.

## The dwarf on the ice

> *The player objected that a dwarf just turning up was too convenient. The scene was retold.*

On the second day the dogs howled at a lone figure following them. Kiri was off her sledge at once: the priests used lone figures as bait. Temur said the mirror was louder now, closer.

> *The player chose: Pallando, well travelled and saying little about it, calls out "What news from the Red Hills?"*

The figure went very still. "Not many Men know the Red Hills." He was Borin Ironfist, sent by his king after miners kept wandering into the deep tunnels and never coming back. He'd followed the last ones' tracks for three weeks. And he'd found a shard of black glass on a frozen miner's body. It was whispering, and he'd been following its pull.

> *The player chose: destroy it. Anything made by Sauron will always betray you.*

Borin's mattock bounced off it. Pallando's blue fire burned it to black dust with a thin scream. "It heard that," gasped Temur. "It knows we're coming."

## The lake

Steam over the ice, a black glass tower on an island, and hundreds of taken people standing still in the snow. Every head turned towards them.

> *The player chose: none of the options. A cleverer plan. Pallando distracts them with speech carrying magic, then sings them all into a deep sleep, because everyone still has to sleep.*

Pallando spoke of home: smoke in a felt tent, a mother calling at dusk, sledges coming in. Faces flickered. Then he sang of rest, and the crowd sank onto the ice in waves and slept. But holding hundreds asleep took nearly all he had.

> *The player chose: Borin looks at the rock.*

"The lake isn't warm from magic," Borin called. "There's fire-rock under here. And old tunnels. Dwarf-cut." They ran straight under the lake to the island.

> *The player chose: the tunnels.*

## Under the lake

They left the hunters guarding the sleepers and went down. In a round chamber they found Borin's missing miners, some long dead, three still alive and digging, mechanically, a new passage up towards the island. "It's using them to dig itself a way out." The mirror spoke through one of them: "Welcome, wizard. You're just in time."

> *The player chose: Pallando sings the dwarves to sleep. The crowd above wakes and pours into the tunnel. Kiri and the hunters hold the narrow passage without hurting anyone, while Pallando and Temur go on.*

"They're our people! Nobody gets hurt! Just hold them!" The press faltered as the climbers moved further from the mirror.

> *The player chose: go into the tower together, Pallando with eyes shut, Temur as his eyes.*

## The second mirror

The mirror showed Temur his grandfather alone in his tent and promised the cold would stop if he knelt, just for a moment. But Batu was smiling. "He never smiles like that. It doesn't know him. It's just guessing." The hold cracked.

> *The player chose: the blow is Pallando's burden alone, whatever it costs.*

Pallando opened his eyes. The mirror showed him Valinor and told him he'd been forgotten, sent East to die unsung. "Perhaps. But I'm not doing this to be remembered." He struck with everything he had. The mirror burst into black dust. Out on the ice, hundreds woke calling names. In the tunnel a man stared at Kiri: "Sister?" It was her brother. Three dwarves asked Borin what on earth he was doing there.

Pallando lay still, his staff cracked down the middle.

> *The player chose: they carry him to the shore.*

A day and a night by the fire, a feather at his lips. Borin wouldn't sleep. Temur held the broken staff, and at dawn saw the crack glowing faintly and closing. "You're holding that very tightly," Pallando croaked. He lived, hair gone white, moving like a very old man.

## A different war

> *The player's direction: this is one of the last times Pallando fights Sauron head on. From now on he fights by gathering the peoples.*

"He can make mirrors faster than I can break them," Pallando told Temur. "What he can't make is people who refuse to kneel." Kiri went home to teach the Hiru to recognise the whispers. Borin went back to the Red Hills with his miners and a message: the Dwarves weren't alone. Temur stayed as Pallando's apprentice and his eyes. Word spread across the north: the mirrors could be broken.

## Where we left off

Finished. Five mirrors remain. Next for the Blue Wizards: Alatar in the south, and the gathering at Rhûn.
