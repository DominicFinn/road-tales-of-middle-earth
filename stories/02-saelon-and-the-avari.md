# The Road to the Unwilling

*The far East, late First Age, after the Nirnaeth.*

## The company

Saelon was an old Noldo loremaster, sick of endless war. The Green-elves of Ossiriand spoke of Elves still living far beyond the Blue Mountains, untouched by Morgoth's war or the Oath of Fëanor. He wanted to find them, partly to ask for help, partly to see what Elves were like before everything went wrong.

He took a Green-elf guide, Dannil; a Dwarf of Belegost, Narvi Stonehand, who was mainly in it for the maps; and Aerin, a young Man of the House of Bëor whose family had died in the Dagor Bragollach.

In the eastern foothills they found a ring of standing stones carved with Elvish runes older than any in Beleriand, and in the middle, a campfire still warm.

> *The player chose: look for more evidence, then follow the tracks.*

A flint arrowhead, not metal. Barefoot tracks, so light they barely touched the ground. They followed them east into a forest that went on forever. At night there was soft singing in the trees that stopped whenever they called out.

## Ithrë

On the ninth night Aerin woke to find a wild-haired Elf crouched by the fire, dressed in bark and leaves, eyes shining like a cat's, studying their faces. She said one ancient word, and Saelon caught his breath: it meant "the Followers", the first Elves' name for Men. She had never seen a human before.

> *Aside: the player asked why the main characters were women. Fair point noted; they chose to keep Ithrë as she was.*

Saelon spoke the oldest words he knew. She laughed at his old-fashioned accent. Her name was Ithrë, and her people called themselves the Unwilling, who refused to follow Oromë West. Something had come out of the dark further east: Men who hunted Elves for a shadow. Her people hid. But "the old ones will want to see a Follower who travels with Elves".

> *The player chose: go with her.*

## The lake

She led them to a wide, still lake full of reflected stars. Not Cuiviénen, long since lost, but so like it the Avari had gathered there. Elves came out of the trees with bows half drawn, and an ancient lord, Morwë, asked why the West had finally remembered them. Saelon told the truth: Beleriand was burning. "First," said Morwë, "you must prove you're not his."

> *The player chose: a song of courage and truth.*

Saelon sang the truth of the Noldor. Not the glories of Valinor, but the Trees, the theft of the Silmarils, the Oath, the Kinslaying, the long war. He didn't hide the shame. Then he sang of Fingolfin riding alone to Angband. Some drew back at the Kinslaying. Some wept at Fingolfin. "A servant of the Dark would have sung only of your glory," Morwë said. "I believe you."

Then torches appeared on the far shore.

## The light of the Trees

> *The player's point: Saelon is one of the Calaquendi. He has nothing to fear from these Men.*

Saelon walked out alone. A hundred Easterlings, led by a sorcerer-captain with a red eye on his shield. Saelon threw back his hood and his eyes blazed with the memory of silver and gold. Easterlings dropped their torches and ran. The captain chanted a binding. Saelon laughed and sang, and the chant cracked, and the sorcerer fell to his knees with his hands over his ears.

> *The player chose: they don't kill him.*

Questioned by the fire, the sorcerer, Khamûl the Lesser, said Morgoth didn't care much about the Avari themselves. He wanted them never to join the Elves of the West, so both could be beaten one at a time. Morwë realised their isolation was exactly what Morgoth wanted. They let the sorcerer go, on foot, with a message: "The Elves of the East and the Elves of the West have found each other again."

Morwë would not lead his people West. But he sent his best archers back with Saelon, led by Ithrë.
