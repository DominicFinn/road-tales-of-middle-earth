# The Mirror in the Hills

*The East, Second Age, long before Gandalf came to Middle-earth.*

## The stranger in the blue cloak

Darun was a fisherman's son on the shore of the Sea of Rhûn. His people were ruled by priests who served a distant lord they called the Giver. The Giver sent iron weapons and took their sons for his armies. None came home.

One stormy night an old man in a sea-blue cloak, with a staff of pale wood, asked for shelter. He called himself Pallando, and asked about the Giver, and a tall dark-haired man with eyes like burning coals. That night the priests came to the door with spears.

> *The player chose: help him escape.*

Darun led him through his grandfather's smuggling tunnel to the boat sheds. In the storm Pallando stood in the bow and spoke one word, and the wind bent round them. "I was sent," he said, "with a companion, Alatar, to find the one your priests call the Giver. His real name is Sauron." Alatar had gone south a year before and sent no word.

At dawn on the far shore they found a great camp of young men being drilled by soldiers in black, and among them Darun's brother Tarek, taken three years before.

> *The player chose: don't shout. Watch, and see where they go.*

The young men weren't chained. They drilled eagerly. "It's not force," Pallando murmured. "It's a voice." The column marched southeast. In a gorge Pallando found a small blue star carved fresh in the rock: Alatar's mark for danger, go no further.

> *The player chose: heed the warning.*

In a crack in the cliff they found a blue crystal. Alatar's voice: the armies lead to a fortress where Sauron's servant has built a great mirror of black glass, and whoever looks into it is bound. He'd tried to break it and failed, and was held there. Don't come unless you can break the mirror first.

"What if someone went in," said Darun, "who couldn't see it?"

## The blind Elf

> *The player's idea: a blind Elf warrior in the hills to the northeast. Villagers leave him food out of pity. They say he was once a great warrior from somewhere far away.*

They found him carving wood by touch outside a cave. "A Man, and one of the Maiar," he called out. "It's been a long time since I smelt the West on anyone." He was Rúmil, one of the Avari, who'd fought in the first wars against Morgoth's servants and been blinded by a sorcerer. "So they finally need the blind Elf. Why would I help the West, which left my people behind?"

> *The player chose: Pallando persuades him. Not for the West, but for the Avari still hidden in the East, who'll have no choice when Sauron's armies turn on them.*

"I had a sister," Rúmil said at last. "She went south to the lakes when I was blinded. I never knew if she lived." He unwrapped a long sword, still bright. "I haven't held this since before the Sun first rose."

## The fortress

On a moonless night they climbed to a high window. Rúmil silenced two guards before they knew he was there. In the great hall stood the mirror, taller than three men, black and rippling, with rows of young men kneeling before it, Tarek among them. Alatar hung chained to a pillar beside it, eyes screwed shut.

A voice from the shadows: "Welcome, Pallando." One of the Maiar, long fallen, in a robe of shifting grey.

> *The player chose: Pallando faces the Maia alone, a battle of wills, while the other two go for the mirror.*

The two spirits locked gazes. Candles guttered and the floor cracked between them. Darun led Rúmil across the hall, eyes down. The mirror whispered to Rúmil, but its whispers needed eyes. He found a crack near the base, struck there before by Alatar, and drove his ancient blade into it. The mirror screamed and shattered.

> *The player chose: find the other wizard.*

Rúmil split Alatar's chains. The grey Maia dissolved into smoke and fled. All across the hall the young men woke. Tarek stared at his hands: "Darun? How long have I been here?"

## The Star-folk

> *The player chose: Rúmil returns with them as a hero and stays a while, teaching lore, and what fear really is.*

The freed men came home across the sea. The priests had fled. The village built Rúmil a house by the water. He taught them the stars before the Sun, Cuiviénen, tracking by sound, the Avari ways of fishing (reed nets that never rot, humming fish into the shallows), herbs, storm-riding boats, the stars for steering. Above all he taught them that Sauron's power was in whispers and promises, and that the bravest thing was to refuse a gift with chains on it.

Travellers came to call them the Star-folk, and they turned away Sauron's priests at spear-point. When Rúmil left at last to look for his sister, Darun, grey-haired, asked if he'd come back. "Elves always come back to the water."

## The map

> *The player asked to go back to the wizards searching the fortress.*

Beneath the hall they found a map of the whole East with seven places marked with a black eye: this fortress, the southern deserts, an eastern island sea, the frozen north, and three unknown names. "Seven mirrors. We broke one." A letter from Sauron spoke of a southern ruler, the Sun-King, promised eternal life for his armies. And a shard of the glass, still whispering.

> *The player chose: destroy it. Nothing of Sauron's can be trusted.*

They burned it on the highest tower with blue fire from both staffs.

> *The player's direction: this is where the real story begins. The peoples of the East must be united to destroy the mirrors.*

The wizards made a pact: each would gather allies from every people they met, and in a year they'd meet at Darun's village on the Sea of Rhûn. Alatar took the south and the Sun-King, where he would find a desert band led by Hazad, the Sun-King's former general. Pallando took the north.
